## 2. Ready-to-paste comment / annotation

This is Propp's "Problem 11" ("intruded Aztec diamonds"), from "Enumeration of matchings:
problems and progress" (1999) -- based on Lior Pachter's observation that a 16x16 square minus 8
dominoes in a corner zig-zag has exactly one tiling. The region (reverse-engineered from the
pixel figure in the primary source, http://jamespropp.org/matchings.pdf, p.270, and verified to
reproduce all 5 previously published terms exactly) is: a 4n X 4n grid of unit cells with an
n-step corner staircase removed -- for k=0..n-1, remove cells (row=4n-1-k, col=k) and
(row=4n-1-k, col=k+1). a(6)-a(12) were computed by the FKT/Kasteleyn method (full skew-adjacency
determinant, multi-modular CRT, exact integer sqrt) and independently verified by TWO further
lineages, all in exact digit-for-digit agreement: (1) an independently-coded anchor using the
explicit closed-form square-lattice Kasteleyn sign rule (horizontal edges +1, vertical edges
(-1)^column) with a direct (non-skew) balanced-biadjacency determinant via plain Bareiss
elimination, reproducing a(1)-a(5); and (2) a second, independently-authored program (different
agent, same explicit sign rule, its own sparse modular-CRT determinant implementation) that
reproduces the full a(1)-a(5) gate and every new term a(6)-a(12) exactly. Propp states explicitly
that "the presence of large prime factors means that no simple product formula is available, and
the analysis will require new techniques" -- this remains a genuine compute-wall extension, not a
restatement of a known formula.
